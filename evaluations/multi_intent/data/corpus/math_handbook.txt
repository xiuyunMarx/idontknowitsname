Unit conversions. 1 dozen = 12 units; 1 week = 7 days; 1 day = 24 hours; 1 hour = 60 minutes; 1 km = 1000 m; 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 liter = 1000 ml. When a problem mixes units, convert everything to one unit before any other arithmetic and carry the unit symbol through every intermediate result.

Percentages. The word percent means per hundred, so p percent of x equals x times p divided by 100. A quantity reduced by p percent equals the original times (100 minus p) divided by 100; a quantity increased by p percent equals the original times (100 plus p) divided by 100. Percentage loss applies to the total before the loss, never to a partial subtotal, unless the problem says otherwise.

Distance, speed and time. Time equals distance divided by speed; distance equals speed times time. When a trip has several legs at different speeds, compute each leg's time separately and add the times; average speed over the whole trip equals total distance divided by total time and is generally NOT the average of the leg speeds.

Money and unit price. Total revenue equals unit price times quantity sold; when sales span several days, compute each day's quantity first, sum the quantities, and multiply by the unit price once, or multiply per day and add — both must agree, which is a useful cross-check. Net savings equal money in minus money out: sum all income and gifts, subtract all spending.

Proportional scaling. When a recipe or mixture for n units must serve m units, every ingredient scales by the factor m divided by n. Compute the factor first as a fraction, then multiply each ingredient amount by it; scaling factors from serving counts are exact fractions and should not be rounded before the final result.

Simple interest. Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, with the rate expressed as a fraction per period and the time in the same periods. The amount after t periods equals principal plus interest.

Worked example, revenue over several days (reference answer). A stall sells lemonade at 2 dollars a cup: Friday 15 cups, Saturday three times as many as Friday, Sunday 10 fewer than Saturday. Saturday is 3 times 15 = 45 cups; Sunday is 45 minus 10 = 35 cups; total cups are 15 + 45 + 35 = 95; revenue is 95 times 2 = 190 dollars. Reference answer: 190.

Worked example, two-leg travel time (reference answer). A cyclist rides 90 km at 30 km per hour and then 40 km at 20 km per hour. Leg one takes 90 divided by 30 = 3 hours; leg two takes 40 divided by 20 = 2 hours; the whole ride takes 3 plus 2 = 5 hours. Reference answer: 5.

Worked example, savings with spending and a gift (reference answer). Leo saves 12 dollars a week for 6 weeks, giving 72 dollars; he spends 25 dollars on a book, leaving 47; his aunt gives him 30 dollars, so he has 47 plus 30 = 77 dollars. Reference answer: 77.

Worked example, percentage loss on planted stock (reference answer). A nursery plants 2 greenhouses, each with 25 trays of 20 seedlings: each greenhouse holds 25 times 20 = 500 seedlings, so 2 times 500 = 1000 in total; a fungus kills 20 percent, which is 1000 times 20 divided by 100 = 200; the surviving stock is 1000 minus 200 = 800. Reference answer: 800.

Worked example, recipe scaling (reference answer). A sauce for 6 portions uses 300 ml of stock; for 9 portions the factor is 9 divided by 6 = 1.5, so the stock needed is 300 times 1.5 = 450 ml. Reference answer: 450.

Sign-off checklist for numeric answers. Before signing off: confirm the final number answers the exact quantity asked, not an intermediate; confirm every given quantity was used or explicitly declared irrelevant; confirm units are consistent and stated; recompute the final arithmetic step once in the opposite order as a cross-check.
