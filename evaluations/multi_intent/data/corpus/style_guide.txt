Core rules. Prefer active voice; keep sentences under 24 words; one idea per paragraph; open with the point instead of throat-clearing; avoid buzzwords such as synergy, leverage and cutting-edge; when a length target is given, stay within ten percent of it.

Register, formal. No contractions; no exclamation marks; full salutations and closings; hedged requests phrased as questions rather than instructions; concrete dates instead of vague references such as soon or shortly. Formal register is the default for external correspondence with clients and sponsors.

Register, neutral. Contractions are acceptable; tone is direct and warm but businesslike; humor is avoided; the reader is addressed as you. Neutral register suits product descriptions, documentation and most internal writing.

Register, casual. Contractions and light humor are welcome; sentence fragments are acceptable for rhythm; exclamation marks are allowed sparingly. Casual register suits social posts and community announcements, never contractual or financial content.

Correspondence. Every professional email states its request in the first two sentences, gives the reason in one short paragraph, and closes with one concrete next step and a date. When asking for an extension or a favor, name the cause factually without assigning blame, state the new date you are committing to, and say what the reader needs to do, if anything.

Product descriptions. Lead with the benefit to the named audience, then support it with at most three concrete specifications; numbers beat adjectives — 40 hours of battery says more than long-lasting; end with the situation in which the product fits the reader's life, not with a slogan.

Blog introductions. An introduction earns the next paragraph: open with a hook the audience recognizes from their own work, make one clear promise about what the post will show, and keep the introduction under the stated length target; the final sentence should point forward into the body of the post.

Social announcements. Announcement posts state what shipped, who it is for, and one proof point such as production usage or scale; invite a concrete action such as trying it or contributing; avoid hashtag pileups — two at most — and avoid marketing superlatives; upbeat is welcome, salesy is not.

Revision checklist. Before sign-off: verify every required content point from the brief appears in the text; verify the register matches the brief throughout, with no formal phrases in casual copy or contractions in formal copy; verify the length is within ten percent of the target; verify the opening states the point and the closing gives the next step or forward hook the brief calls for.

Common fixes. Replace passive constructions with the actor as subject; split any sentence past 24 words at its conjunction; delete adverbs that add no information such as very, really and extremely; replace vague quantities with the number from the brief; move the point of the piece into the first sentence if it is buried.
