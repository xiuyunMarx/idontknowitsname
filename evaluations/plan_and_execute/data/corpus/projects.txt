Tidewater Survey. The Tidewater Survey is the institute's flagship estuarine monitoring effort and is currently in the fourth year of a five year mandate that runs from 2021 through 2026. Its scope covers three estuary channels on the northern shore, each of which is transected eleven times per tidal cycle so that the survey can resolve how suspended sediment concentration, channel cross section, and bank position co-vary between spring and neap conditions. The survey is led by Dr. Nadia Ferreira and is administered by the Coastal Dynamics Lab, which supplies its field crews, its small boat time, and its share of the shared archive. All of its optical measurements are taken with the Halcyon spectrometer, which the survey books for roughly one hundred and forty instrument days per year; this dependency is the survey's single largest scheduling risk, because every day the spectrometer spends in maintenance is a day of transects that cannot be recovered within the same tidal window. Financially the survey draws on the Blue Horizon Program, which reimburses field costs quarterly in arrears and requires that a compliant deposit exist before a claim is paid. As of the most recent oversight review the survey is on schedule against its transect target but is running eight percent behind on archive deposits, a gap the lab attributes to the reprocessing that followed the 2024 calibration drift. Its principal outputs are a quarterly sediment flux bulletin, an annual channel migration atlas, and the raw transect series itself, which is the product most requested by outside groups. The survey has never been suspended, though it was paused for nineteen days in 2024 while the Halcyon spectrometer was recalibrated, and the resulting gap in the series is flagged in every derived product.

Lantern Array Study. The Lantern Array Study characterises how the phase stability of large coherent light source arrays degrades over observation runs lasting a hundred hours or more. It began in 2018, is led by Dr. Ivo Marchetti, and is administered by the Photonics Lab. Its measurements are taken with the Perihelion laser bench, on which it holds a standing weekly reservation, and it is financed by the Meridian Optics Grant. The study's distinguishing feature is that it treats the bench's own vibration environment as a measured variable rather than a nuisance, and it therefore maintains a parallel record of building disturbance that has proved useful to other groups working in Vaughn Tower. Its results are released annually as a stability atlas, with the underlying interferograms held back for a twelve month embargo. The study has twice extended its mandate, most recently through 2024, and a further extension is under discussion contingent on the grant's renewal.

Saltmarsh Census. The Saltmarsh Census produces an annual inventory of vegetation cover, channel geometry, and habitat class across four protected marshes. It began in 2022, is led by Dr. Selma Okonkwo, and like the Tidewater Survey is administered by the Coastal Dynamics Lab and financed by the Blue Horizon Program. Its measurements come from the Wader drone fleet, flown in six sorties per marsh per season, and its classification pipeline is retrained each year against a fixed set of ground control quadrats. Because it shares both a lab and a funding program with the Tidewater Survey, the two efforts compete directly for field crew time in the late summer, and the census is the effort that yields when that conflict arises.
