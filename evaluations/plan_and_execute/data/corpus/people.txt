Nadia Ferreira. Dr. Nadia Ferreira is a senior research fellow at the Aldermere Research Institute and the lead investigator of the Tidewater Survey. She joined the institute in 2019 after eight years of estuarine fieldwork at a coastal agency, where she built the sediment transport model that she has continued to develop here, and she holds a doctorate in physical oceanography awarded in 2011. She is a member of the Coastal Dynamics Lab and sits on the institute's archive committee, a position that matters for the survey because she is the person who negotiated the current retention exemption for raw transect series. Her published work concerns sediment transport across tidal flats and the seasonal migration of channel banks, and she is the corresponding author on the survey's channel migration atlas. Colleagues describe her management style as unusually protective of instrument time; she was the investigator who pushed for the quality report requirement on Halcyon spectrometer deposits after the 2024 calibration drift, over objections that it would slow deposits down. She supervises two postdoctoral researchers and a rotating field crew of four, and she carries the survey's compliance responsibility under the Blue Horizon Program, which means the quarterly reimbursement claims are signed by her.

Ivo Marchetti. Dr. Ivo Marchetti joined the Aldermere Research Institute in 2016 and holds the rank of principal investigator. He is a member of the Photonics Lab and leads the Lantern Array Study. Colleagues credit him with the alignment procedure that the institute now applies to all of its interferometric benches, a procedure that halved the time required to qualify the Perihelion laser bench after a mount slip. His research concerns coherence in extended optical arrays, and he has argued in institute forums that the twelve month embargo on interferograms is longer than the field now expects.

Ingrid Sollberg. Dr. Ingrid Sollberg directs the Coastal Dynamics Lab and has done so since 2017. Her role is administrative rather than investigative: she allocates field crew time between the Tidewater Survey and the Saltmarsh Census, signs the lab's annual report to the Blue Horizon Program, and represents the lab on the institute's instrument scheduling board, where the Halcyon spectrometer's booking calendar is settled each quarter. She has held that the lab's eight percent archive deposit shortfall is a consequence of the reprocessing burden rather than of crew capacity, a position the oversight board has accepted twice.
