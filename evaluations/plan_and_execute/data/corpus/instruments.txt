Halcyon spectrometer. The Halcyon spectrometer resolves the composition of suspended and dissolved material from reflected light across four hundred spectral channels between three hundred and eleven hundred nanometres. It is housed in Brackwater Hall, was commissioned in 2015, and is maintained by the Optics Support Team, which holds it to a fourteen day service cadence during the field season and a twenty eight day cadence outside it. The instrument has three documented failure modes, and every group that books it is expected to know them. The first is calibration drift in the blue channels, which appears as a slow upward bias in the retrieved chlorophyll proxy and which is the failure that forced the nineteen day pause in the Tidewater Survey during 2024; it is detected by comparing the daily white reference against the rolling thirty day median and is corrected by a full lamp recalibration. The second is condensation on the fore optic during rapid temperature transitions, which produces a broadband loss of signal that is easily mistaken for high turbidity; the mitigation is a thirty minute equilibration period before the first transect of a cold morning, and crews that skip it are the most common source of rejected data. The third and least common is a shutter timing fault that duplicates the previous frame, which is invisible in single frames and only shows up as an implausibly high frame to frame correlation in the quality report. Because two of the three modes corrupt data silently rather than stopping the instrument, the Optics Support Team requires that a quality report accompany every deposit, and the archive will reject a deposit that arrives without one.

Perihelion laser bench. The Perihelion laser bench provides a vibration isolated platform for long baseline interferometry and has been in service in Vaughn Tower since 2013. It is maintained by the Optics Support Team on a twenty one day cadence. Its dominant failure mode is thermal creep in the reference arm, which manifests as a slow phase ramp that can be mistaken for genuine array drift; the standard control is a nightly closed loop null measurement, and any run without one is treated as unqualified. A secondary and rarer fault is a mount slip after building works, which is why the bench is re-surveyed whenever construction is scheduled in the tower.

Wader drone fleet. The Wader drone fleet consists of six fixed wing aircraft configured for low altitude multispectral survey and was commissioned in 2020. It is housed in Thorne Pavilion and maintained by the Field Robotics Crew. Its failure modes are mechanical rather than optical: airframe fatigue at the wing root, which is tracked by sortie count and retires an airframe at twelve hundred sorties, and magnetometer interference during launch from vehicles parked too close to the launch rail, which corrupts the attitude solution for the first minute of flight. The fleet's maintenance log is the reference the Saltmarsh Census cites when it explains coverage gaps.
