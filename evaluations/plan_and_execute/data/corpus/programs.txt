Blue Horizon Program. The Blue Horizon Program sponsors sustained observation of shoreline and estuarine change, carries a budget of 4.2 million, and runs from 2020 through 2026 under the Coastal Trust Foundation. It finances the Coastal Dynamics Lab, and through the lab it funds both the Tidewater Survey and the Saltmarsh Census. Its funding conditions are the strictest of the institute's three major programs and are the conditions an oversight board is most likely to ask about. Costs are reimbursed quarterly in arrears rather than advanced, so the lab carries roughly one quarter of field expense on its own balance at any moment. A claim is payable only against data that has been deposited in the institute archive in a compliant state, which since 2024 means accompanied by an instrument quality report; a deposit that the archive rejects does not count toward the claim, and this is the mechanism by which the survey's eight percent deposit shortfall translates directly into a delayed reimbursement rather than a mere paperwork issue. The program requires an annual report signed by the lab director, currently Dr. Ingrid Sollberg, and it reserves the right to audit any instrument booking that it has reimbursed. It does not permit its funds to be used for instrument capital purchase, only for operation and maintenance, which is why the Halcyon spectrometer's recalibration in 2024 was charged against institute reserves and not against the program. Renewal is decided in the final year on the basis of the deposit record rather than of publications, a criterion the program adopted after two earlier efforts left no usable archive behind.

Meridian Optics Grant. The Meridian Optics Grant supports the development of precision optical measurement capability, carries a budget of 2.8 million, and ran from 2019 through 2024 under the Vance Photonics Endowment. It finances the Photonics Lab and thereby the Lantern Array Study. Its conditions are lighter than the Blue Horizon Program's: costs are advanced annually, deposits are required within eighteen months rather than at claim time, and capital purchase is permitted up to a fifth of the budget. Its renewal is under discussion, and the Lantern Array Study's extension depends on the outcome.

Deep Earth Initiative. The Deep Earth Initiative supports investigation of crustal structure and deep rock composition, carries a budget of 6.5 million, and runs from 2015 through 2027 under the National Geoscience Board. It finances no coastal work and is included here only because its archive rules were the template from which the institute's own retention schedule was drawn.
