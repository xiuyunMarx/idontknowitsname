Existing agent serving systems exploit the predictability of agentic workloads to guide KV-cache scheduling but only at a binary prefix--suffix granularity. We observe that agent prompts have finer schedulable structure, a property we call \emph{intra-prompt heterogeneity}: they can be decomposed into multiple heterogeneous components that differ in sharing pattern and lifetime. This property creates opportunities to enhance KV-cache management by exposing more prefix reuse and scheduling KV-cache more precisely. Meanwhile, frameworks that let LLM invocations be declared as language constructs emit prompts programmatically, making intra-prompt heterogeneity analyzable from program code. To this end, we present \sysname, a compiler-serving co-design that recovers and exploits intra-prompt heterogeneity for efficient KV-cache management. Our evaluation shows that \sysname reduces average JCT by 1.40$\times$ and average TTFT by 1.62$\times$ on geometric mean over vanilla SGLang, and by up to 1.90$\times$ and 4.49$\times$. On a mixed workload, it raises throughput by 1.51$\times$ on geometric mean and by up to 1.86$\times$.
