LLM agent frameworks increasingly expose model invocations through structured programming abstractions, with framework runtimes lowering program-level constructs into concrete prompts. We observe that these programmatically generated prompts are inherently heterogeneous: their components exhibit different sharing scopes, lifetimes, and evolution patterns across invocations. However, existing serving systems treat prompts as opaque text, losing this structure and missing opportunities for more effective KV-cache reuse and management.

We present \sysname, a serving layer that recovers and exploits prompt heterogeneity for efficient KV-cache management. \sysname lifts incoming prompts against framework templates to recover call sites and binding boundaries, learns binding evolution patterns and application-level control flow from execution traces, and uses this information to optimize KV-cache behavior at two levels. Within a request, \sysname reorders bindings by lifetime to expose stable, reusable prefixes. Across requests, a lifecycle-aware planner proactively prefetches prefixes likely to be reused and evicts cache entries with low future utility. We implement \sysname on SGLang and evaluate it against multiple established serving systems. \sysname achieves up to $1.4\times$--$1.7\times$ lower mean JCT and $1.7\times$--$1.75\times$ lower mean TTFT than vanilla SGLang.
