\section{Evaluation}
\subsection{Enviromental Setup}
\paragraph{Configurations} We evaluated \sysname on a single RTX-3090 with Qwen-8B(bf16). \sysname is implemented on SGLang with HiCache with chunked-prefill~\cite{agrawal2024sarathi} enabled. For Baseline comparison, we selected vanilla SGLang and two opeaqe requests serving systems CacheScout and Continuum \cite{zhang2026cachescout, li2025continuum}.

\paragraph{Workloads.}
\paragraph{Workloads.}
We evaluate \sysname using two representative agent applications: a fact-checking agent and a coding agent. Using these applications, we execute two benchmarks: HoVer~\cite{jiang2020hover}, a multi-hop fact verification benchmark requiring evidence collection and reasoning across multiple Wikipedia articles, and HumanEval~\cite{chen2021evaluating}, a code-generation benchmark that evaluates the functional correctness of generated programs using unit tests.

\subsection{Evaluation Analysis}
Our evaluations on \sysname address the following research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How much does \sysname improve end-to-end serving performance?
    \item How much does two scheduling mechanisms of \sysname contribute to the improvement?
    \item How do the two mechanisms of heterogeneity-aware KV-cache scheduling in \sysname interact and reinforce each other?
\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection{RQ1: How \sysname improve end-to-end performance}
We evaluate \sysname on two representative agent applications: a fact-checking agent on the HoVer dataset and a coding agent on the HumanEval dataset. We use mean job completion time (JCT) and mean time to first token (TTFT) as the end-to-end performance metrics.

\paragraph{JCT}
As shown in Figures~\ref{fig:jct-coding}, \ref{fig:jct-fact}, \ref{fig:speedup-coding}, and~\ref{fig:speedup-fact}, \sysname consistently reduces mean JCT over the vanilla SGLang baseline, achieving $1.3\times$--$1.7\times$ speedup on the fact-check agent and $1.1\times$--$1.3\times$ on the coding agent. On the fact-check agent, Continuum performs comparably to SGLang at low concurrency but its advantage vanishes as load increases, whereas \sysname sustains its gains across all concurrency levels. On the coding agent, \sysname outperforms every other system; CacheScout and Continuum degrade sharply at high concurrency and fall behind SGLang.

\paragraph{TTFT}
\sysname also lowers mean TTFT across all workload configurations (Figures~\ref{fig:ttft-coding} and~\ref{fig:ttft-fact}). On the fact-check agent, the reduction is substantial at every concurrency level, while Continuum and CacheScout offer little or no improvement over SGLang. On the coding agent, \sysname matches or beats all baselines up to a concurrency of 40; at the highest concurrency, KV-only achieves a slightly lower TTFT.

The improvement of \sysname comes from more efficient KV cache scheduling. From figure ~\ref{fig:cache-status}, it can be cleared observed that performance gain comes from lower KV cache miss rate. \sysname's Heterogeneity-aware KV Cache scheduling helps keeping useful KV entries in cache. This scheduling mechanism incorporates two aspect, heterogeneity aware prompt re-layout and Prediction-guided KV Management. We thus investigate into detailed serving status to understand how the two kv scheduling mechanisms contributes to performance improvement. 

\paragraph{Speed-up introduced by heterogeneity-aware prompt re-layout}
To investigate the effects of prompt re-layout, We conduct an ablation experiment which disables \sysname's KV scheduling mechanism, and only use heterogeneity-aware prompt re-layout, as shown in the \emph{re-layout only} arm in ~\ref{fig:cache-status}. In serving code agent, which is a decode heavy agent, re-layout gains nearly all optimization when concurrency is less than 28. But when workload continues to grow, re-layout itself cannot sustain useful KV, cache miss rate begins to drop, leading to larger gap between it and full \sysname. Across sessions of fact check agent, re-layout only keeps a steady slight more cache miss rate, which caused a performance deficiency compared to full \sysname. 

These evaluation results clearly demonstrate that heterogeneity aware prompt re-layout itself helps enhancing performance. Across all workload pressure, heterogeneity-aware prompt re-layout improves performance by introducing more opportunities of kv sharing, thus reduced cache miss and optimizes serving performance. It could even achieve full performance \sysname in some cases, but is still behind full \sysname under workload pressure.

\paragraph{Effects of Prediction-guided KV Management}
Adopting similar methodology, we create a ablation arm that only activate KV management of \sysname, which is the KV-only arm in figure ~\ref{fig:cache-status}. This mechanism shows a substantially distinct behavior with prompt re-layout. In fact check agent serving sessions, it only works under low pressure, where $c \le 14$, which is very similar to other kv management frameworks: Continuum and CacheScout also work only under very low concurrency level. Moreover, this arm, together with Continuum and CacheScout, even stay inert in serving code agent. 

The reason for this phenomenon is quite straightforward: KV cache management needs space to operate. Prediction-guided KV Management's effects come from keeping useful entries in cache, prevent them from being evicted. However, when pressure is high, ongoing requests competes for memory, none kv cache can survive. Consequently, KV cache management's effect diminishes under high workload pressure. Continuum and CacheScout can also be observed with similar phenomenon. But effect of Prediction-guided KV Management of \sysname disappear much later than Continuum and CacheScout. In fact check agent, Continuum and CacheScout lose their functionality at $c=10$, while \sysname's Prediction-guided KV Management survives to $c=14$.

We analyze the two mechanism's interactions and co-reinforcement, we scrutinize kv cache statistics and investigate into some representative pressure points to figure out. We first analyze the behavior pattern of \sysname under high workload. 

In serving code agent, \emph{re-layout only} obtains same performance as full \sysname. But under high workload pressure, full \sysname obviously works better. Enabling \sysname's kv cache management mechanism furthermore improves serving performance. Comparing the cache status in figure \ref{fig:cache-coding}, \emph{re-layout only} keeps same cache miss rate initially(When $c \leq 28$) but device miss increases. Host tier catches the mistakenly evicted kv entries. \emph{re-layout only} has same cache miss rate but higher device miss rate. The efficient host-to-device kv cache loading algorithms in SGLang fill the gap, therefore performance doesn't diverge at those points. When concurrency grows to more than 32, full \sysname begins outperforming \emph{re-layout only}. It has less cache miss, leading to better performance. 

This behavior also emerges when testing on the fact check agent. As we mentioned above, \sysname's kv management algorithm only works under low pressure, where $c \le 14$. Heterogeneity-aware prompt re-layout has effect across all pressure because it creates more kv sharing opportunities, which leaves space for kv scheduling. Therefore, heterogeneity-aware prompt re-layout and prediction-guided KV management are not isolated two mechanisms, prompt re-layout create spaces, and kv management further improves cache efficiency.

We selected some representative workload pressure and investigate how many tokens are recomputed. We choose $c=12, 28, 40$ for code agent and $c=8,14,18$ for fact check agent. As shown in \ref{fig:cdf-coding}, prompt re-layout maintains exactly the same recomputed tokens as full \sysname, but for c=40, things are different, re-layout only is left behind, adding kv scheduling reduces tokens recomputed furthermore. In the regime of 
fact agent, all kv scheduling mechanisms reduce the recomputed tokens. CacheScout, Continuum and \sysname all enhance cache efficiency. Similarly, when workload is high, \sysname's prediction-guided KV management could add optimization upon the heterogeneity aware prompt re-layout, but kv scheduling itself cannot take effect.
