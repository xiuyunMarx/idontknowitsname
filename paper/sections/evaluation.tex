\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
In this section, we evaluate \sysname on multiple representative agent workloads, and ablate each components.
\subsection{Environmental Settings}
\subsubsection{Testbed and Model}
We conduct all experiments on one NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU using Qwen3-14B-AWQ. We allocate 5.2\,GB of GPU memory to the device KV-cache pool and 10\,GB of CPU memory to the host KV-cache pool.

\subsubsection{Baselines}
We compare \sysname against the following serving systems, together with two ablated variants of \sysname. We also run each established serving system with \sysname's adaptive prompt re-layout enabled:
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Vanilla SGLang}~\cite{zheng2024sglang} uses SGLang's HiCache with LRU eviction. 
    \item \textbf{CacheScout}~\cite{zhang2026cachescout} identifies agents from their request prefixes and learns inter-agent execution transitions online. It uses the learned transition patterns to prioritize cached KV entries and prefetch the prefixes of predicted successor agents.
    \item \textbf{Continuum}~\cite{li2025continuum} retains each agent program's KV cache for an adaptively selected time-to-live (TTL). The TTL is derived from observed inter-invocation delays and expected cache reuse, allowing the system to preserve reusable context while the agent executes tools.
    \item \textbf{KVFlow}~\cite{pan2025kvflow} uses an Agent Step Graph to build a workflow-aware KV cache management framework for agentic workloads.
    \item \textbf{\sysname-KV-only} enables proactive KV-cache scheduling but disables adaptive prompt re-layout.
    \item \textbf{\sysname-Relayout-only} enables adaptive prompt re-layout but replaces proactive KV-cache scheduling with LRU eviction.
\end{enumerate*}


\subsubsection{Workloads}
We evaluate \sysname on four representative agent workloads, written with ByLLM~\cite{dantanarayana2025mtp} in Jac: a HoVer-style~\cite{jiang2020hover} fact-checking agent, a HumanEval~\cite{chen2021evaluating} coding agent, a BFCL~\cite{patil2025bfcl} tool-use agent, and a FinanceBench~\cite{islam2023financebench} financial-analysis agent. We further evaluate a mixed workload comprising all four agent types to assess \sysname's end-to-end performance under concurrent, heterogeneous demand. The mixed workload runs 20 concurrent lanes, five per agent type; when a lane finishes a session, it immediately starts the next one.

We tested \sysname under different number concurrent workflows, and a mixed workloads. Across these workloads, we compare average job completion time(JCT), average time-to-first-token(TTFT) and throughput(session/min) for the mixed workload.

\paragraph{Performance on mixed workload}
As shown in table~\ref{tab:mixed-workload}, \sysname raises throughput to 1.44$\times$ that of Vanilla SGLang and reduces average TTFT and JCT by 34\% each. Continuum and CacheScout perform close to Vanilla SGLang on all three metrics. The gain of \sysname comes from a higher cache hit rate in both device and host memory.

\paragraph{Average JCT}
Across the four workloads, \sysname reduces average JCT by 6--48\% compared with Vanilla SGLang (Figure~\ref{fig:e2e_speedup}), and by 34\% on the mixed workload (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-workload}). When concurrency is low, adaptive prompt re-layout accounts for nearly all of the gain. As concurrency grows, the gain of re-layout alone shrinks; proactive KV-cache scheduling carries the gain: it evicts volatile tails first and protects the reusable heads that predicted calls will need. On fact check, scheduling contributes from the lowest concurrency.

Except for an 8--13\% reduction on BFCL at $c=4$, Continuum and CacheScout perform close to Vanilla SGLang. Both exploit predictability between invocations but treat each prompt as an opaque token sequence. In the original prompt order, volatile bindings often precede reusable ones, making prefixes hold little that later calls can reuse. \sysname-KV-only, which schedules on the original prompt order, shows the same limit.


\paragraph{Average TTFT}
Across the four workloads, \sysname reduces average TTFT by 12--78\% compared with Vanilla SGLang (Figure~\ref{fig:e2e_speedup}), and by 34\% on the mixed workload (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-workload}). As with JCT, adaptive prompt re-layout accounts for nearly all of the gain at low concurrency, and proactive KV-cache scheduling carries it as concurrency grows. On fact check and BFCL, and on finance at low concurrency, the TTFT reduction exceeds the JCT reduction, because a reused prefix skips its prefill, whereas JCT also includes decoding and tool execution which the KV-cache does not shorten. Continuum and CacheScout show the same limit as in JCT.

\paragraph{Task Quality}
We therefore compare task accuracy between the five systems that serve the original prompts and the that serve the re-laid prompts, using a paired permutation test over inputs (Table~\ref{tab:quality}). On all four workloads the difference is not significant ($p \geq 0.66$) and no consistent direction of change. The performance gains of \sysname therefore do not come at the cost of task quality.

\subsection{Cache Efficiency of \sysname}
Figure~\ref{fig:hit_rate} shows the cache hit rate of prompt tokens at the same concurrency levels as Figure~\ref{fig:e2e_speedup}. On coding and BFCL, adaptive prompt re-layout alone brings the hit rate at low concurrency to that of \sysname: it places the stable and shared bindings at the front, where they form a reusable head that later calls hit. As concurrency grows, the hit rate of \sysname-Relayout-only drops quickly, because LRU eviction does not distinguish reusable heads from volatile tails. With proactive KV-cache scheduling, \sysname keeps a higher hit rate than \sysname-Relayout-only as concurrency grows, and on coding its hit rate stays nearly flat. On fact check, \sysname's hit rate exceeds that of \sysname-Relayout-only from the lowest concurrency. Continuum and CacheScout stay close to Vanilla SGLang at every concurrency. \sysname-KV-only stays well below \sysname, since under the original prompt order the reusable head it can protect is short. The mixed workload shows the same pattern (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-workload}).

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
We ablate \sysname on the mixed workload by enabling its components one at a time (Table~\ref{tab:mixed-workload}). Besides \sysname-KV-only and \sysname-Relayout-only, \sysname w/o prefetch keeps adaptive prompt re-layout and proactive KV-cache scheduling but disables prefetch. Figures~\ref{fig:e2e_speedup} and~\ref{fig:hit_rate} show \sysname-KV-only and \sysname-Relayout-only across concurrency levels.

Adaptive prompt re-layout alone reduces JCT by 22\% compared with Vanilla SGLang and raises throughput to 1.18$\times$. Adding proactive KV-cache scheduling without prefetch reduces JCT by a further 12\% and raises throughput to 1.32$\times$. Prefetch reduces JCT by another 5\% and brings throughput to 1.44$\times$.

The two mechanisms depend on each other. Applied to the original prompt order, proactive KV-cache scheduling reduces JCT by only 7\% (\sysname-KV-only); applied after re-layout, it reduces JCT by 16\% relative to \sysname-Relayout-only. The combined 34\% reduction is larger than the sum of the two individual reductions. Re-layout places the stable and shared bindings at the front, so a served prompt divides into a reusable head and a volatile tail, and scheduling gets a reusable head to protect.
